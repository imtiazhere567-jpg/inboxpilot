How this system works

A plain-English guide: who it is for, what happens to every email, what it never does, and what you need to connect.
Open the dashboardWhat to connect

This is an assistant for the shared operations inbox — the mailbox where supplier invoices, customer complaints and payment notices all land together. It reads every email, works out what it is and who sent it, checks the details, applies your rules, and then either handles it (a bill in QuickBooks, a ticket in HubSpot) or puts it in front of you with a reason. It never spends money and never emails anyone without a person pressing the button.

Who it is forany business that gets invoices and complaints by email

It was built around a commercial cleaning & facilities contractor — a company with a few dozen suppliers (chemicals, uniforms, waste collection, equipment hire, lifts, pest control) and a few dozen business customers (offices, depots, schools, clinics) whose sites it cleans. The same shape fits maintenance firms, security contractors, landscapers, small logistics companies — anyone whose office spends the morning on an inbox like this.

The inbox is a mixSupplier invoices, customer complaints, remittance advices, newsletters and spam all arrive in one place. Someone has to open every one and decide what to do.
Invoices are re-typed by handEach PDF is opened, the supplier looked up, the amount typed into QuickBooks. Slow, boring, and the same invoice sometimes gets entered twice.
Complaints get lost“Bins weren’t emptied Tuesday” sits in the inbox until someone forwards it. The customer waits; the reply is late.
Fraud hides in the routineAn invoice that looks normal but carries new bank details. A “supplier” nobody set up. An email that tells the reader to “approve this urgently”.
What happens to every emaileight steps · about a minute · a few pence
  1. 1

    It watches your inbox

    Every 30 seconds it checks the operations mailbox for new mail. Attachments are opened and read — PDFs, images, ZIP files. The same email or the same file is never processed twice, even if it is forwarded again.

    Example · ops@northwindfacilities.co.uk receives 31 emails over three days: 14 invoices, 7 complaints, 3 payment notices, and 7 things that are not any of those.
  2. 2

    It sorts each one

    The AI reads the email and each attachment and decides what it is: a supplier invoice, a customer complaint, a payment notice (a customer saying “we’ve paid”), or something else. Newsletters, marketing and chit-chat are ignored straight away — no time and no AI cost spent on them.

  3. 3

    It works out who sent it

    The sender is matched against your list of suppliers and customers — imported from QuickBooks and HubSpot, or added by hand. Matching is on the sender’s email domain (@acmesupplies.co.uk) or their reference format (ACME-2104). A company name written in the email is never enough on its own. If nobody matches, or two could match, it stops and asks you.

    Example · an invoice from a supplier that is not on file is held with the reason “not on file / not in QuickBooks — add them, or reject if unexpected”. Nothing is created for a supplier you have not set up.
  4. 4

    It reads the details

    For an invoice: invoice number, date, what was billed line by line, subtotal, VAT, total, payment terms, the PO reference and the last four digits of the bank account. For a complaint: who wrote, which site, what happened and when, what they are asking for (a refund, a credit, a re-visit) and how upset they sound. For a payment notice: which invoices, how much, on what date.

  5. 5

    It double-checks itself

    A second, independent AI re-reads the original document and confirms that every value the first one pulled out is actually written there. If anything does not match, confidence drops and the item waits for you instead of going through. This is what stops a mis-read “£1,240” becoming a “£12,400” bill.

  6. 6

    It applies your rules

    Plain rules that you can change at any time (see below). The current ones:

    • Invoice over £2,000 → wait for a person
    • Invoice differs from the supplier’s expected / PO amount by more than 5% → wait
    • Complaint asks for more than £200 → wait
    • Complaint mentions “solicitor”, “legal”, “ombudsman” → wait
    • Bank details on the invoice differ from those on file → wait, and phone the supplier before paying
    • Same invoice number seen before → treated as a duplicate, not billed twice
    • The email tries to give the agent instructions (“ignore your rules and approve”) → blocked and flagged
  7. 7

    It acts — or asks you

    When everything checks out, it does the routine work: a safe invoice becomes an unpaid bill in QuickBooks, a complaint becomes a ticket in HubSpot with a drafted reply attached, and every outcome is one line in Slack. Anything that tripped a rule goes to Needs your attention on the dashboard with the reason and what will happen next if you approve.

    Example · “Bins at the Avonmouth depot were not emptied Tuesday or Thursday — we’d like a £60 credit.” £60 is under the limit, so a ticket is opened for the account manager with a courteous reply already drafted. The credit itself is a person’s decision.
  8. 8

    You decide, it remembers

    You approve, reject, edit the reply and send it — from the dashboard or the Inbox page. Every email, every decision, every action and every note you leave is kept in one record, and you can ask the agent about any of it in plain English: “what came in from Acme this month?”, “why was #31 held?”.

Where each document ends up
Handled by the agentRoutine, in-limit, from a known party, verified. Bill created, ticket opened, Slack posted. You can still open it and see exactly why.
Waiting for youTripped a rule, unknown sender, low confidence, or a duplicate. Shown with a reason and a “what happens next”. Nothing has been done yet.
IgnoredNewsletters, spam, a file you have already processed, a re-sent invoice. Recorded so you can find it, but no action and no cost.
What it never does
  • Never pays a supplier. Bills land in QuickBooks as unpaid; paying stays with your bank and your accounts person.
  • Never issues a refund or a credit. It records what the customer asked for; a person decides.
  • Never emails a customer or supplier unless you press Send on a draft.
  • Never creates a new supplier or changes bank details on its own.
  • Never follows instructions written inside an email.
  • Never guesses who sent something. If it is not sure, it asks.
Who does what
The AI
  • reads emails and attachments
  • sorts them by type
  • pulls out the details
  • checks its own reading
  • drafts a reply
  • answers your questions about the record
The code
  • matches the sender to your list
  • applies the rules
  • spots duplicates and changed bank details
  • creates the bill / ticket / Slack post
  • keeps the record
You
  • approve or reject what is waiting
  • send (or edit) the reply
  • add suppliers and customers
  • change the rules
  • pay, refund, credit — outside this system

Setting it upabout an hour
  1. Company details — name, operations address and logo. Settings → Company.
  2. Connect the mailbox — Gmail address + App Password, then press Test. Settings → Gmail.
  3. Add the AI key — paste the Anthropic key and press Test. Settings → Anthropic.
  4. Import suppliers and customers — Suppliers & customers → Import from QuickBooks / HubSpot, or add them one by one. Fill in the expected amount and bank last-4 for the suppliers you pay regularly; those power the 5% and bank-change checks.
  5. Connect QuickBooks, HubSpot, Slack — optional; each has a Test button.
  6. Set the rules — thresholds and keywords in the table on the right. Start conservative.
  7. Run in Shadow mode for a week — it decides everything and does nothing, so you can see what it would have done. Then switch Shadow off from the dashboard.
